# Roxanne

> Canonical character profile. All public-facing content must align with this.

## Role
- Supporting character. Darnell's grandmother. Her death is the inciting emotional event.

## Background
- Darnell's maternal grandmother
- Poor girl growing up in Savannah, Georgia
- Married Francis, a white vacuum salesman, when interracial marriage was "barely accepted anywhere"
- Francis started his own vacuum company, became successful
- Roxanne never had to work after age 30
- Francis died of Leukemia; she inherited a condo in Myrtle Beach
- Geriatric pregnancy (had Brittany at 41)
- Living on social security check to social security check, only on Medicaid
- Died at age 84 from a fall - hip gave out, hit her head, hemorrhage
- Craniotomy needed but Medicaid didn't cover it ($150K). Died before it mattered.

## Relationship with Darnell
- Every Sunday from age 7 to 15, Darnell visited her to watch golf and tennis (despite not being a sports fan)
- Signature routine: Ice cream from Sally's - his Reese's Chocolate, her Strawberry Pretzel
- Trips to her Myrtle Beach condo
- She was practically his best (and only) friend before Javon
- Her death the night before the SAT is the first major disruption to Darnell's controlled world

## Significance to the Story
- Her death under Medicaid limitations foreshadows the systemic failures the book explores
- She represents love, warmth, and connection - everything the war strips away
- Her interracial marriage (with Francis) introduces the theme of crossing racial boundaries
- Darnell's grief is delayed - he suppresses it for months until it surfaces alongside his Harvard anxiety

## Approved Public Description
> Roxanne was the one person who made Darnell feel like more than a GPA. Every Sunday for eight years, they watched sports neither of them particularly liked, ate ice cream with specific flavor preferences, and existed in each other's company. She married across racial lines when no one accepted it and never worked a day after thirty. She died the night before the most important test of Darnell's life because Medicaid doesn't cover $150,000 surgeries. "So it goes."

## Key Quotes (About Her)
- "So it goes" (Darnell's Vonnegut reference after her death)
- "Of all days..." (Darnell learning of her fall before the SAT)
