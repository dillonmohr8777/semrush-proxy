# CIA Director Norman Newman

> Canonical character profile. All public-facing content must align with this.

## Role
- Supporting antagonist. The institutional enforcer of Garnier's plan.

## Background
- CIA Director under President McNulty
- Staunch conservative
- Made a career of increasing surveillance on Black neighborhoods
- Continued Reagan-era policies of community upheaval against Black populations
- Agency was in cahoots with top nationwide drug dealers: supplied them with drugs, threatened life in prison, forced them to keep selling, took 50% of profits
- Avoided IRS scrutiny because "who's going to truly monitor another government agency when the entire system is unchecked?"

## Personality
- Initially resistant to Garnier's plan (thought it was too extreme)
- Won over after Garnier threatened his life at gunpoint
- Quickly became an "eager participant" and "shameless executioner" once committed
- Hired 112 new leadership personnel to run the Black Intellects surveillance program
- Described as "not actually the brightest person in the world" despite running intelligence

## Key Actions
- Accompanied Garnier to Russia to plan the false-flag attack
- Coordinated CIA agents to scout and track intelligent young Black men
- Ordered Alec's memory erasure via NeuroClick chip
- Showed Alec the secret escape door in the Spanish facility (out of guilt)
- Planted a mole officer among the 34 "RATS" who planned to revolt
- Present during Javon's interrogation in Spain

## The Institutional Machine
Newman represents the institution itself - the CIA as a weapon of racial suppression dressed up as national security. His drug programs, surveillance operations, and the Black Intellects mission are all extensions of the same system. He's not Garnier's level of evil, but he's the machinery that makes it possible.

## Approved Public Description
> The Director of the CIA built his career on the quiet suppression of Black communities. Surveillance programs. Drug supply chains disguised as law enforcement. When the world's richest man came to him with a plan that made everything he'd done look modest, he said no. Then a gun was pointed at his head. Then the money appeared. Then the plan started to make sense. Norman Newman is the system's middle manager - not the architect, not the face, but the man who makes it all run on time.
